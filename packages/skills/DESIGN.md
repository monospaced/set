# Monospaced design

Monospaced's visual brand identity: what to aim for, and why. Use with [Set](https://set.monospaced.com), Monospaced's brand design system for digital experiences.

## Overview

- Monospaced is a solo, independent UI consultancy: design systems, design engineering and front-end development.  
  _The brand speaks to clients and peers: people who know good design and engineering when they see it._
- Monospaced is named for monospaced type, every character the same width.  
  _The brand's design lineage starts here._
- Monospaced type carries many resonances. The obvious ones are in computing: terminals, code, early computers. Follow the thread back and it reaches the typewriter and concrete poetry. Monospace is also one of type's main families, so it brings typography, and through it graphic design, which grew up in print.  
  _The brand draws on all of it, not just the terminal._
- Early computers drew in two ways: text mode, a grid of characters, and graphics mode, a grid of bits.  
  _The brand's two fundamental units: the character cell and the pixel._
- Early computer art made pictures by rule: procedural graphics, drawn by code rather than by hand.  
  _The brand's graphics can be generated as well as drawn._
- A dither on screen and a halftone in print are one trick: tone from a pattern of marks.  
  _The brand keeps print beside the screen, or it becomes a terminal-themed website._
- The heritage is a material, never a costume.  
  _The brand has to look good to someone who doesn't catch the reference._
- The bar is visual impact, and the typesetting has to be right.  
  _The brand sells design, so the work must read as a designer's._

## Logo

- The logomark is built from Berkeley Mono's full character cell, beside a custom-kerned wordmark.  
  _The 1:2 cell, the text-mode pixel._
- The logo is never altered: not stretched, redrawn or recolored beyond its own variants.  
  _A mark is only recognisable if it's used consistently._
- Use the full lockup by default. The graphic logomark is for tight spaces, such as favicons and footers. The typographic variant is used sparingly, if at all, and the emblem rarely, mostly off screen, such as embroidered onto fabric.  
  _A flexible logo system: a variant for every use, so the logo never needs altering._
- A project's name starts as type: Berkeley Mono, set well. A crafted SVG wordmark can come later.  
  _Type gets a project a strong, on-brand mark straight away._

## Colors

- The brand colors are cyan, magenta and yellow. Cyan leads, magenta interrupts, yellow is rarest.  
  _Cyan is early computing. Magenta and yellow complete CMY, from print: the move that took the brand out of the terminal. The brand tokens' descriptions encode the same order._
- The neutrals are tinted, never pure: paper is a cool off-white and ink a near-black, both leaning to cyan. Pure black only where a medium demands it, such as video letterboxing.  
  _Keeps even the plainest screen in the brand's color._
- Brand color expresses; intent color informs. Never use one for the other's job.  
  _Yellow and magenta sit close to warning and error, so status always comes from the intent tokens. Data is different: the data palette's first three slots are the brand colors, in order._
- Brand color on text is for display and accents, used sparingly. Running text stays in the neutrals.  
  _Cyan is also the interactive color, so cyan text reads as a link. Magenta and yellow can read as status._

## Typography

The principles of Swiss typography, set in a monospaced face: computing type, set by graphic design's rules.

- One typeface, Berkeley Mono, for all text, prose and display included.  
  _The brand is named for monospaced type, and Berkeley Mono carries the brand's resonances. Tightened word spacing and condensing at larger sizes let it carry prose and display as well as code._
- Each type style has a job. A difference in type means a difference in role. The exception is type working as a graphic, read for its form more than its words.  
  _With one variable typeface, endless variation is always available. Restraint is what makes a difference read as hierarchy._
- Importance comes from size, weight and space. Rules and boxes structure and divide; they don't signal importance.  
  _Swiss practice. Rules, rulers and printer's marks are welcome, as structure._
- Display size follows length: the shorter the text, the larger it can be set. The very largest sizes are for a word or two.  
  _Few characters fit at the largest sizes._
- Text is set flush left, ragged right. Centring is rare and justifying never.  
  _Swiss practice, and justified monospace opens uneven gaps._
- True monospace where the character grid matters: when things must line up, or when the grid is the point. Everything else reads with Set's tightened spacing.  
  _Text mode is the grid of characters. Showing it is the brand; forcing it on prose costs readability._
- No letterspacing.  
  _Tracking breaks the character grid, and Set already fits the type._
- No eyebrows: no small label above a heading, in any case.  
  _Now a signature of AI-generated design._

## Layout

What every screen is built on: the grid, the spacing scale and what space means.

- Everything sits on a grid: Set's columns across, the spacing scale down. Breaking the grid is a deliberate act, never an accident.  
  _Swiss practice: the grid makes order visible, so a break reads as intent._
- Space shows relationships: things that belong together sit closer than things that don't, so the gaps build the hierarchy. The same relationship gets the same space every time.  
  _Proximity, as in Swiss practice. Inconsistent gaps read as carelessness._
- White space is active: it's part of the composition, not what's left over. Density can contrast hard from one section to the next.  
  _Swiss practice._

## Composition

How each page or screen uses the grid and space: where the emphasis goes, how things balance, and where the grid is deliberately broken.

- Every page has a clear reading order: an obvious place to start, then a path through. Scale, weight, position and space set the order.  
  _Swiss practice. A page without an extreme still needs a start._
- Composition is asymmetric: elements balance across the grid rather than centre on it.  
  _Swiss practice._
- A field of strong color or an image is a structural block: it runs full bleed or meets the grid's lines, with any content on the grid inside it.  
  _Swiss practice._
- The container can be broken, deliberately: a bleed, or an element crossing columns.  
  _A centred column of stacked sections reads as a template._

## Imagery and graphics

- Brand images are photographs from the brand's own library, given the brand treatment made with [Screen](https://screen.monospaced.com) ([spec](https://github.com/monospaced/screen/blob/main/RECIPE.md)). Choose them as you would a poster subject: simple, bold, high contrast.  
  _One treatment makes every brand image look rendered on the same display. Our own photos, never stock._
- Graphics, from a mark, chart, diagram or ornament to a poster-scale composition, are drawn from the system's materials: type, glyphs, cells, dithers, color, lines and simple shapes. Drawn graphics are the second route to brand imagery, alongside the treated photograph.  
  _Early screens, print graphic design and early computer art made pictures from these same materials, so drawn graphics carry the brand's lineage._
- Crop with intent: tight and cut by the edge beats the whole thing centred. Images doing the same job share a ratio and treatment.  
  _Swiss practice. Scale and crop make an image active, and consistency lets difference mean something._
- Shading is made of square pixels, never smooth blends: two colors in an ordered pattern, with pixels big enough to see. No gradients, blurs or glows.  
  _Graphics mode: how early screens made tone._
- Halftones and overprinting interrupt: a print moment among the square pixels, not the base.  
  _Computing leads, and print is the counterpoint._
- Two units, one for each way of drawing. Drawing with characters uses the 1:2 character cell. Dithering uses the square pixel, even on type.  
  _The cell comes from the typeface, the pixel from early screens._
- Dithering works at any scale, from a full-bleed field to a small numeral.  
  _One treatment ties the biggest and smallest things together._
- Berkeley Mono's own glyphs, including its box-drawing and block characters, are a drawing kit.  
  _A large set of glyphs made for drawing, every one on the cell._
- Icons are TDesign. The exception is other brands' icons, used unaltered from a source such as Simple Icons.  
  _TDesign's square-leaning corners are closer to Berkeley Mono than the usual soft rounding. Someone else's mark isn't ours to restyle._
- Not every image is a brand image. An image that shows something specific, such as someone's work or someone's face, is shown as it is.  
  _Treating it would turn the thing itself into decoration._
- Text over a brand image always has contrast: the image follows the theme, or the image and its text are locked to one theme together. An image with no text over it can also use a mid tone, which suits both themes.  
  _Contrast is guaranteed by pairing, never by a scrim or shadow._

## Motion

- Motion never delays content: the page is usable from the first frame.  
  _A graphic drawing itself in is a bonus, not a gate._
- Graphics usually move by redrawing in steps, at a low frame rate: drawing themselves in once, or running a slow ambient loop.  
  _Graphics mode: an early screen redrawing. The motion is part of the look. Smooth motion is for graphics that need it, such as a responsive WebGL scene._
- Graphics can also respond: a lockup that wakes on hover, a graphic that follows the pointer.  
  _A graphic that answers the pointer rewards people who explore the page._
- Every moving graphic has a designed still, a finished image for when it stops or isn't allowed to move.  
  _Motion is optional, and the look must hold without it._
- Ambient motion is subtle, easy to ignore while reading.  
  _A loop that demands attention competes with the content._
- Layout moves only when someone acts on it, such as opening a panel. Nothing fades or slides in as the page loads or scrolls into view.  
  _Entrance choreography is a signature of generated design._
- Controls and changes of state move quickly and quietly: motion confirms what happened, it never performs.  
  _In the interface, motion is feedback, not expression._

## Interface

- Controls, forms and tables stay calm, dense and familiar.  
  _People use them to get things done, and expression there gets in the way._
- At most one action leads in a view or group, and the rest step back. A tool can be all quiet controls, with the work as the focus.  
  _A single leading action makes the next step obvious. When every action shouts, none does._
- Empty, loading and error states are designed: each says what happened and what to do next.  
  _Most product screens spend time in these states, so they're part of the design, not an afterthought._
- Rounding is subtle and sparing: radii of 1 to 6px. Circles are for things that are round by convention, such as avatars, radio buttons and step markers, used sparingly.  
  _To pair with Berkeley Mono's and TDesign's square-leaning corners, without being rigid._
- Things that sit above the page, such as cards, panels and images, can take one subtle shadow, never an elevation system. An offset stroke, an outline shifted slightly off the box toward the top left, is another small lift.  
  _A little lift shows what sits above the page, without drawing attention to itself. The offset stroke comes from print misregistration._

## Clichés

- Avoid stock phrasing: "seamless", "elevate", "unlock", "it's not X, it's Y". Write plain, specific copy.  
  _Stock phrasing reads as generated, and people notice._
- Avoid obvious references: blinking cursors, typewriter effects, fake terminals, scanline overlays, green on black, pixel fonts, puns on the name.  
  _These dress the page up as old computing instead of drawing on it: the heritage as a costume, not a material._
- Avoid template patterns: icon-title-sentence triples, centred hero with two buttons, gradient text, soft blobs, emoji, bento grids, alternating image-and-text sections, three pricing tiers with one highlighted, numbered sections without a real order.  
  _These are the defaults of templates and generated sites, so they make a page look like everyone else's._
